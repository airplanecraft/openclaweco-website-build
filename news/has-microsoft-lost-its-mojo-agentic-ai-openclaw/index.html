Has Microsoft Lost Its Mojo? Agentic AI Push and OpenClaw to the Rescue

June 5, 2026 | 3 Min Read
#copilot#github#openclaw#claude-code#ai-agents

This week, Satya Nadella kicked off Microsoft’s annual Build developer’s conference with typical boasts about new products and a sunny view of AI. The clear focus of his speech was how Microsoft was going gaga for "agentic AI." Yet, a distinct cloud loomed over the gathering in San Francisco’s Fort Mason—and we aren’t talking about Azure.

While the valuations and share prices of its core competitors have soared, Microsoft’s stock has faced pressure this year. Its workplace AI products, which like just about everything at Microsoft these days are branded under the Copilot umbrella, have seen disappointingly slow uptake. Crucially, while the company was an early pioneer in AI coding tools, Anthropic has grabbed the lead with its groundbreaking agentic approach to coding via Claude Code. In a defensive maneuver, Microsoft went as far as ending internal Claude Code licenses to force its own developers to use #Copilot.

Meanwhile, GitHub, the invaluable code repository and Microsoft subsidiary, has suffered unprecedented downtimes that have led longtime fans to complain and even defect to alternative platforms. One Reddit post asked outright: "Has #GitHub become a dumpster fire?" For Microsoft, losing the hearts and minds of the coding community would amount to a catastrophe, threatening former CEO Steve Ballmer’s famous mantra: "Developers! Developers! Developers!"

Enter Scott Hanselman, a Microsoft VP on the GitHub technical staff who has spent years evangelizing developer tools. Late last year, he was considering leaving the company after 18 years to teach high school science. But in November, he became supercharged by the agentic coding revolution kicked off by Claude Code and OpenClaw. He helped bring the latter, which is open-source, directly into Microsoft. At the Build conference, he took the stage during Nadella’s keynote, demonstrating how the company’s "copilots" could automate complex tasks for coders and enterprise workers alike.

When questioned about the recent GitHub outages, Hanselman explained that the platform is currently battling an unprecedented flood of bot traffic, comparing it to the early days of email spam. He asserted that GitHub is successfully scaling to meet this challenge, dismissing the outages as a temporary hiccup on a platform that still boasts over 99% uptime.

[AgentUpdate Depth Analysis] Microsoft's aggressive embrace of agentic AI and the open-source OpenClaw framework at Build highlights a pivotal shift in the AI Agent ecosystem. Faced with the disruptive rise of Anthropic's Claude Code and fragile developer trust due to GitHub outages, Microsoft is pivoting from purely closed-source Copilot paradigms to embracing open-source agentic frameworks. This is a clear indicator that the next phase of the AI war is moving beyond static LLM capabilities to dynamic, multi-agent orchestration. The long-term impact on the AI Agent ecosystem is profound: standardizing open frameworks within massive enterprise suites will lower the barrier to custom agent deployments, challenging proprietary dominance and forcing a highly collaborative, open-ecosystem approach across major tech giants.
