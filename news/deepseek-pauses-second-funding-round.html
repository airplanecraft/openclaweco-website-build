DeepSeek Reportedly Pauses Second Funding Round of $1.4B Amid Trust Concerns

July 27, 2026 | 3 Min Read
#deepseek#venture-capital#large-language-model#ai-infrastructure

According to a report from Bloomberg, DeepSeek (#DeepSeek-AI), the rising Chinese large language model star, has reportedly hit the pause button on its massive second external funding round. Sources close to the matter revealed that DeepSeek has verbally notified potential investors that the investment agreements, which were scheduled to be signed within the next few days, will not be signed as planned. The round targeted raising at least 10 billion RMB (approximately $1.47 billion USD) at a pre-money valuation of at least 480 billion RMB ($71 billion USD), representing a 40% jump from its first-round valuation of 50 billion USD completed just weeks ago.

The unexpected pause occurred just days after a rumored internal communication memo leaked online, raising concerns within DeepSeek. Sources suggested that the suspension is partly due to "trust issues" that emerged during the investment discussions. However, the transaction is not officially terminated. DeepSeek may still reboot the process in the future, and negotiations remain fluid.

For a long time, DeepSeek relied heavily on proprietary funding from High-Flyer Quant, founded by Liang Wenfeng, and only opened up to external capital in 2026. In June 2026, DeepSeek closed its massive first external round of 50 billion RMB (approx. $7.4 billion USD), making it one of the largest private tech deals in China. In that round, Liang pledged 20 billion RMB of personal funds, while Tencent and CATL planned to commit 10 billion RMB and 5 billion RMB respectively, alongside state-backed funds.

DeepSeek launched the second round immediately after the first to secure more funds to support compute infrastructure and business expansion. Although renowned for high model efficiency and low inference costs, maintaining a competitive edge in the frontier AI race demands immense capital for AI chips, data centers, and power grid access. There is currently no sign that the pause was due to weak investor demand or valuation disagreements.

The sudden halt leaves three major questions unanswered: First, when will this multi-billion dollar round resume, and under what terms? Second, will this trust crisis alter how DeepSeek vets and engages with institutional investors? Third, will this delay impact its IPO trajectory? Prior reports indicated that DeepSeek had already started preparing for its initial public offering, aiming to file as early as late 2026. A prolonged funding pause could reshape its pre-IPO valuation benchmark and liquidity strategies.

[AgentUpdate Depth Analysis] DeepSeek's sudden funding pause highlights the delicate balance between rapid capital scaling and ecosystem trust in the frontier AI race. From a technical perspective, DeepSeek has established a formidable moat in the open-source and AI Agent community due to its highly efficient MoE architecture and disruptively low API pricing. Countless downstream Agent developers using frameworks like LangChain and CrewAI rely on DeepSeek as their go-to engine. Any developmental stall caused by financial friction or trust issues would inevitably trigger a domino effect across the AI Agent startup ecosystem. Compared to OpenAI's strictly controlled capital path, DeepSeek's situation underscores a vital lesson: building state-of-the-art models requires not just compute and algorithms, but also a resilient, transparent corporate governance structure to secure long-term ecosystem stability.
