OpenAI's Jia-Yi Weng Proposes New Paradigm for Agentic AI: Beyond Gradients, Towards Autonomous Task Completion

May 9, 2026 | 3 Min Read
#agentic ai#harness engineering#agentic workflows#ai agents

OpenAI's post-training engineer, Jia-Yi Weng, has recently proposed a significant new paradigm hypothesis for #Agentic AI. This hypothesis suggests that the future evolution of Agentic AI may transcend reliance on training ever-larger models or traditional gradient-based optimization methods. Instead, the focus is shifting towards AI agents that can explicitly and autonomously complete complex tasks, moving beyond mere question-answering to intelligent planning and multi-step execution.

This "beyond gradients" training paradigm is seen by many in the industry as the next crucial direction for AI development. It posits that AI agents will exhibit enhanced autonomy and adaptability in specific scenarios, even enabling them to autonomously read, hypothesize improvements for, modify, and execute code, akin to human engineers. This shift could give rise to new roles like "prompt engineers," who guide AI tasks through meticulously crafted prompts rather than parameter tuning.

Related concepts such as "Agent #Harness Engineering" are emerging, signaling a paradigm shift and ecosystem restructuring for AI agents by 2026. This includes "#Agentic Workflows" task runners capable of breaking down and allocating complex tasks. Furthermore, new human-AI collaboration paradigms like "Reversed Conversation," as proposed by Chorus, where AI and humans actively communicate and collaborate to achieve goals, are gaining traction. These developments underscore a deeper level of interaction between AI agents and human collaborators.

Market interest in Agentic AI is also surging, exemplified by the introduction of the Agentic AI ETF (AGIQ), highlighting AI Agents as a burgeoning investment hot spot. Practical applications, such as Tencent's QClaw AI assistant, have been upgraded to integrate with WeChat, offering remote operation and content generation capabilities, thereby lowering the barrier to entry for AI assistant adoption. Leading institutions like Anthropic are also publishing trend analyses on "Agentic Coding Trends," reinforcing the substantial potential of agents in code generation and optimization.

These collective advancements mark a "century evolution" for AI, moving from statistical modeling to proactive Agentic AI. This heralds the full arrival of the AI Native era, poised to profoundly impact organizational transformation, business models, and the very definition of artificial intelligence.
