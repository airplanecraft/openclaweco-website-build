OpenAI Pushes Back on Apple Trade Secret Lawsuit

July 15, 2026|3 Min Read
#openai#apple#hardware#ai-companion

OpenAI pushed back Tuesday against allegations made by Apple in a trade secret lawsuit, suggesting the complaint lacks merit. "While we take these allegations seriously, we’re not aware of any evidence that this complaint has merit," #OpenAI said in a statement. "We believe in fair competition and allowing people the freedom to work wherever they choose, and we’re focused on building innovative technology that empowers people everywhere."

The statement comes several days after #Apple filed a lawsuit against the AI lab, alleging that OpenAI employees, who previously worked at the iPhone maker, engaged in a coordinated effort to obtain confidential information and intellectual property. The 41-page complaint, filed in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, contains a string of allegations against OpenAI leadership, including Chief #Hardware Officer Tang Tan. Before joining OpenAI, Tan was a veteran at Apple, where he worked for 24 years and held top positions, including vice president of product design for the iPhone and Apple Watch.

This is the first time OpenAI commented on the case itself. In its initial statement hours after Apple filed its lawsuit, it proclaimed a lack of interest in technology developed by other companies, telling TechCrunch: "We have no interest in other companies’ trade secrets. We remain focused on building innovative technology that empowers people everywhere."

Apple claims in its lawsuit that its internal investigation uncovered evidence that OpenAI and its partners used the company’s confidential information as it develops its own hardware product. Reports, along with OpenAI’s recent acquisition of Jony Ive’s startup io, suggest the company is working on a device that could directly compete with Apple’s business. Bloomberg reported on Tuesday that OpenAI is working on a mobile, screen-free smart speaker, described by people familiar with the plans as a “humanlike AI companion” designed to live in the home.

The device reportedly has no screen, includes moving mechanical elements, and is being built with input from several former Apple engineers who worked on the iPhone and Mac, making Apple's hardware anxieties highly tangible.

[AgentUpdate Depth Analysis] The legal battle between Apple and OpenAI over hardware talent and trade secrets signals that the AI battleground has officially transitioned from cloud models to physical edge devices. OpenAI’s rumored screen-free "humanlike AI companion" represents a paradigm shift for AI Agent delivery. Unlike Apple's screen-centric ecosystem or traditional smart speakers, a screenless, mechanical agent aims to interact naturally via voice and embodied movements, embedding itself into daily lives. By stripping away screens, OpenAI is building an interface custom-tailored for agentic workflows rather than app browsing. However, as OpenAI leverages former Apple veterans to accelerate its hardware ambitions, navigating Apple's formidable IP moat will be the ultimate test. This lawsuit is not just an IP dispute; it is the opening salvo in the battle to define the physical interface of the AI Agent era.
