Carney Warns of AI "Model Risk," Comparing Anthropic Ban to 2008 Crisis

June 15, 2026 | 3 Min Read
#anthropic#model-risk#ai-governance#g7#mcp

Former central bank governor Mark Carney has warned that the US export restrictions forcing Anthropic to suspend its Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models expose a dangerous systemic vulnerability: global over-reliance on a handful of powerful proprietary AI models. Speaking in Ireland, Carney framed the shutdown as a structural warning rather than the failure of a single firm.

Drawing a direct parallel to the 2008 financial crisis, Carney—who previously led both the Bank of Canada and the Bank of England—emphasized that the tech industry now faces a similar "model risk." Just as the collapse of Lehman Brothers revealed catastrophic interconnectedness in banking, concentrated AI dependence poses systemic risks. To mitigate this, Carney called for redundancy and diversity in AI infrastructure, mirroring the regulatory principles imposed on post-crisis financial systems.

Carney noted that while there is an active flow of information between Canadian and US regulators regarding #Anthropic’s risk profile, the bigger picture is structural. The warning comes just ahead of the G7 summit in France, where AI governance tops the agenda. Tech leaders including Anthropic’s Dario Amodei, OpenAI’s Sam Altman, and Google DeepMind’s Demis Hassabis are scheduled to meet with #G7 leaders. Carney indicated that Canada will push for concrete commitments to diversify AI pipelines, building on its recently launched $2.3 billion national AI strategy.

[AgentUpdate Depth Analysis] From the perspective of the evolving AI Agent ecosystem, Carney’s warning regarding "model risk" is a critical wake-up call. Today's most sophisticated agentic workflows are highly coupled with a tiny cohort of frontier LLMs. If a primary model experiences sudden regulatory bans or outages, thousands of dependent corporate Agents would instantly suffer "brain death." To build resilient enterprise Agent fleets, the ecosystem must transition from single-model dependency toward multi-model orchestration, dynamic model routing, and robust open-source fallback options. Adopting open standards like the Model Context Protocol (#MCP) will allow Agents to seamlessly switch backbones, guaranteeing operational continuity and preventing catastrophic single-point failures in the agentic economy.
