Trump Administration Discusses Government Stake in OpenAI

June 6, 2026 | 3 Min Read
#openai#anthropic#ai-policy#national-security

The Trump administration is actively discussing taking a government stake in OpenAI, according to a new report. CNBC noted that these discussions have been ongoing for over a year, with #OpenAI CEO Sam Altman first proposing the concept to President Donald Trump in 2025.

A source familiar with the matter revealed that the agreement could involve OpenAI donating equity to seed a national "Public Wealth Fund," a concept OpenAI previously outlined in a policy proposal. This fund would invest in diversified, long-term assets, allowing all U.S. citizens to benefit from the "upside" of AI growth, potentially through direct payouts. Trump addressed this on Friday, noting that "there are concepts where pieces could be given to the American public, where the American public essentially becomes a partner." Senator Bernie Sanders also confirmed discussing this with Altman, advocating for the U.S. government to take up to a 50% stake in major AI firms.

Governments are increasingly intervening in AI development. Earlier this week, Trump signed an executive order establishing a voluntary framework that allows advanced AI developers to provide the federal government with early access to models for cybersecurity reviews. This process heavily involves the National Security Agency (NSA) and other national security bodies, directing federal agencies to establish an AI cybersecurity clearinghouse and develop classified benchmarking to safeguard critical infrastructure while maintaining U.S. tech leadership.

In contrast, rival Anthropic has called for potential limits on AI development, warning that "recursive self-improvement" might occur much sooner than expected. #Anthropic shared data showing its frontier models have rapidly accelerated their coding, debugging, and research capabilities, potentially sparking a feedback loop where AI tools independently build superior versions of themselves. While Anthropic noted that slowing down would be ideal to address the vast economic implications of uncompetitive human labor, it acknowledged that a unilateral slowdown might simply allow less cautious global actors to catch up, leaving everyone less safe.

[AgentUpdate Depth Analysis] The discussions surrounding a government stake in OpenAI signify a paradigm shift where AI is no longer treated merely as commercial software, but as a sovereign geopolitical asset. The proposed "Public Wealth Fund" structure represents a unique model of state-capital alignment in the AI era. For the broader AI Agent ecosystem, this state-level interest, combined with Anthropic's warnings of "recursive self-improvement," highlights a pivotal tension. As autonomous Agents transition from narrow task automation to self-directed code generation and iterative model optimization, they will inevitably trigger both national security scrutiny and safety firewalls. Future AI Agent frameworks will need to operate within highly regulated, sovereign-monitored environments. Developers must prepare for a future where Agent autonomy is tightly bound by national security benchmarks, transforming compliance from a bureaucratic hurdle into a foundational design constraint for advanced Agent systems.
